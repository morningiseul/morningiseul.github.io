Plus, the filter kept mangling his messages into a jumble of special characters, which pissed him off even more, so he went out of his way to type differently, saying they suuuuuuucked 4ss at the game and that they should either go ahead and slice their useless f4ck1ng head off, since they clearly didn’t have one anyway, or chop their fingers off, because what good were they if they couldn’t even hit the right keys on the g0dd4mn keyboard? But to think that would be his downfall.

A couple of swear words were all it took for them to suspend his main account, hanjihanjang[^1].” Meanwhile, they allowed the bastard who reported him to get off scott-free, even though he was the kind of trash who paid other people to boost his rank for him. They were stupidly quick when it came to these sorts of reports, too. Whenever he sent in an inquiry asking for more variation in skin colours, all he ever got back was an automated response.

“Ugh, right at the end of the season, too.”

He’d been swamped with work this season—photoshoots, advertisements, and whatever else his management decided to pile onto his schedule. The only reason he’d managed to climb this high on the ladder was because he’d carved out time from an already packed schedule to play. He’d skipped every single after-party, regardless of whether he was in Italy or Germany, to rush back to his hotel room and grind ranked matches like his life depended on it. Which also meant that every single game counted. So of course he’d lost his temper when his teammates kept feeding and dragging his rating down.

His main account, which always sat at the highest echelons of the ladder, had been suspended this season right on the threshold of that heavenly tier. Not that he had any intention of taking a thirty-day break from the game, but thinking about the rank of his alternate account left a bitter taste in his mouth. What was it again? Gold? He only ever logged into that account to claim event-exclusive cosmetics. It wasn’t an account he actively played on.

His phone buzzed on the wireless charger beside the keyboard. Jihan closed the infuriating suspension notice and reached for the device.

He wasn’t in a sophisticated enough headspace to feign social grace under meaningless updates right now. The only reason he’d opened the app in the first place was because capitalism demanded it. His real focus lay elsewhere.

After a brief moment of deliberation, Jihan clicked on a match-history website. It opened to his main account, which was his most recent search.

Jihan squeezed his eyes shut, then opened them again and searched a different username. This time, it was his alt account. He wasn’t even sure he remembered the username correctly, but fortunately, a record appeared.

“Wait, what the fuck?”

It was one step away from decaying into Gold from inactivity. Jihan practically threw his phone onto the desk and snatched his keyboard. His fingers flew across the keys as he entered his ID and password.

Anything but Gold. Sure, he’d treated the account like a joke whenever he used it to grind events, throwing matches and never taking the results too seriously, but things were different now that he was forced to play on this account for an entire month.

A pop-up blocked his screen, requesting he change his password since it had been over ninety days. Jihan simply capitalised the first letter of his existing password then logged in, only to immediately let out a groan.

He had no skins.

His character stood in the lobby wearing the dingy default outfit, looking pathetic beyond words. The limited-time cosmetics he’d collected from past events had long since expired.

He couldn’t queue looking like this. Jihan opened the cash shop, clicked the top-up tab, charged his credit card, and immediately bought a server megaphone. The generic outfits sold in the cash shop were ugly and unimaginative, so he’d never even considered them an option in the first place.

Getting his hands on a second gen hanbok would be easy enough. The real problem was the tails. Leaving aside the fact that they started at a staggering 1,500 diamonds, they only dropped from limited-time event loot boxes, which meant the supply was incredibly dry. For reference, the going exchange rate was roughly ten thousand won for a hundred diamonds.

Jihan ended up spending a full two hours camped out in the server chat hunting for tails, and even then he’d only managed to scrounge up two of them. To make matters worse, he’d been fleeced on one of the tails, shelling out an outrageous 3,000 diamonds just to lock it down.

“Fuck me, I’m screwed if any of these characters get banned…”

Zenox was a 5v5 MOBA where two teams battled over five towers and one main base. The standard team composition featured two tanks, one melee DPS, one ranged DPS, and a support. Each match was preceded by a character ban phase, and each team was allowed to ban five characters per match, which meant around ten characters were taken off the table every game. The process of selecting your character was known as the pick phase, and the process of eliminating a character was known as the ban phase. Together, they were simply referred to as the pick and ban phase.

Given that the game boasted over ninety characters, and also taking into consideration the fact that most of the broken champions—the so-called OP characters—were banned by default anyway, ten bans might not have seemed like that big of a deal. The problem was that Jihan’s main role happened to be melee DPS.

Up in the Legend and Master tiers, players banned strategically, targeting counter-picks based on the enemy’s team composition. However, that level of tactical foresight was strictly reserved for Diamond and above. Down in Platinum and below, bans got burnt on popular troll picks, characters that some players personally happened to dislike, or whichever champion had fed the hardest in someone’s last match. And in that Elo, the one eating those spite-bans was almost always a melee DPS.

You could swear up and down that you were different, but what did that matter when no one was willing to believe you? Every melee player claimed they were different, but they were almost always the cause of team toxicity, notorious for throwing the game the second things didn’t go their way.

Jihan hadn’t believed it at first, either. But when he hopped on this account to fool around in a different role, figuring he wouldn’t take it too seriously since it wasn’t even his main, he was slapped with the reality of why melees were considered the usual suspects in this tier.

Those motherfuckers were blind as bats, with zero game sense to speak of. Half of them couldn’t even farm properly, botching their one basic job as a melee DPS. The other half would hoard all the team’s resources, claiming they needed to scale for the late game, ruin the early game for everyone else in the process, and then bitch and moan afterward about how their team never gave them any proper openings to deal damage.

Because of this, players in this tier had gotten into the habit of ditching melee DPS altogether, opting to run double-ranged comps instead. Even if it meant sacrificing some overall damage in the process.

As a result, the odds were high that Jihan’s mains would end up getting banned. And if both characters he’d specifically bought the tails for got banned at the same time? Nothing would wound his pride more. That big, fluffy tail was hanjihanjang’s signature look. Players spent the entire game staring at the backs of their characters, after all, and it just felt wrong, almost unbearable, not to see that fluffy white tail swishing behind him.

Still, there were no other sellers on the market, so he’d just have to make do for now. Once he clawed his way out of Platinum and into Diamond, matches would finally start feeling bearable again. For now, his only goal was escaping this venus flytrap of an Elo.

…And exactly two hours later, that plan was smashed to pieces.

<p style="text-align: center;">***</p>

So, here was Jihan’s current situation.

In his first match, the player who had been assigned to tank had complained that the only tank they knew how to play had been banned and revealed their username in chat and started begging someone to swap roles with them. With the rest of the team clearly reluctant to budge, Jihan decided to take the high road for the sake of a smooth start and gave up his melee DPS slot, locking in tank instead. And honestly, the player who took over his old role turned out to be pretty decent. They farmed well, picked off the enemy’s damage dealers, and even set up a 5v4 team fight at one point.

The real problem was their ranged DPS. They kept whining that no one on the team was peeling for them, then had the audacity to start building defensive items on a damage dealer of all things.

No matter how you looked at it, forgetting your duty as a damage dealer and building defensive items instead of offensive ones early on in the game was the kind of play that deserved to get you flamed into oblivion. Even so, Jihan bit his tongue. He decided to give them the benefit of the doubt, keeping a close eye on the minimap and rotating whenever he could to keep the ranged DPS alive.

The result, however, was that this jackass didn’t have a functioning brain cell to their name. Despite being a ranged DPS, they would constantly front-line ahead of the support and chase low-health enemies all the way into the enemy side of the map without a single mobility skill to bail themselves out.

Then, to top it off, they would flame the support for not following up to protect them. The enraged support would then fire back with, “You stupid fucking ranged DPS, you’re the one who front-lined ahead of me and dived head-first into the enemy camp, and now you want to blame me?”

After that, the game followed its natural course. The team imploded, and Jihan couldn’t solo-carry the game with a tank that dealt no damage even if he tried.

Defeat.
